THE E-WASTE FIELD GUIDE

Know what you’re disposing of.

Explore common electronic waste, understand its possible environmental impacts, and learn how to handle it more safely.

⌕
Safety first: Do not dismantle devices, puncture batteries, or burn cables. Keep damaged or swollen batteries away from heat and seek advice from a suitable collection service.
▯
PERSONAL DEVICES

Mobile phones & accessories

Includes smartphones, tablets, chargers, earphones, USB cables and power banks.

Environmental concern

Phones can contain metals and battery chemicals. Dumping them may waste recoverable materials and, if damaged or improperly processed, pollute soil or water.

Better disposalBack up and erase personal data; separate accessories where practical and use an authorized e-waste channel.
▱
COMPUTING

Computers & peripherals

Laptops, desktops, keyboards, mice, hard drives, monitors and related equipment.

Environmental concern

Circuit boards and screens may contain lead or other hazardous materials. Improper dismantling can expose workers and release contaminants.

Better disposalBack up and securely erase storage; remove accounts and use a reputable recycler or take-back programme.
▰
BATTERIES & POWER

Batteries, UPS & inverters

Rechargeable cells, laptop batteries, power banks, UPS systems and inverter batteries.

Environmental concern

Some batteries contain corrosive or toxic materials and can cause fires if crushed, short-circuited or handled carelessly.

Better disposalNever put batteries in regular waste. Protect terminals where safe and take them to a battery collection point.
▭
HOME ELECTRONICS

TVs & home appliances

Televisions, printers, scanners, refrigerators, washing machines and microwave ovens.

Environmental concern

Some screens and components contain hazardous substances; cooling equipment can contain refrigerants that need specialist handling.

Better disposalUse a suitable take-back or authorized collection service. Do not cut refrigerant lines or dismantle sealed components.
▤
OFFICE EQUIPMENT

Printers & cartridges

Printers, scanners, fax machines, copiers, toner and ink cartridges.

Environmental concern

Plastic housings, circuit boards and leftover toner can become persistent waste if discarded without suitable processing.

Better disposalLook for manufacturer take-back schemes or collection programmes for equipment and cartridges.
⌘
NETWORKING

Routers & network devices

Wi-Fi routers, modems, network switches, servers and related electronics.

Environmental concern

These devices contain circuit boards and metals that can be lost through landfill disposal or released by unsafe processing.

Better disposalReset devices to factory settings and clear configuration data before recycling.
◉
SMALL GADGETS

Cameras, wearables & gadgets

Digital cameras, smartwatches, speakers, headphones, gaming consoles and controllers.

Environmental concern

Small electronics are easy to overlook, yet contain batteries, plastics and recoverable metals that need proper processing.

Better disposalGather small items in a secure container and check whether batteries should be separated by a qualified handler.
⌁
CABLES & ACCESSORIES

Wires, cables & adapters

Power cords, extension leads, adapters, USB cables and electronic connectors.

Environmental concern

Open burning to recover copper can release harmful fumes from insulation and contaminate the surrounding area.

Better disposalDo not burn or strip cables yourself. Submit them to a collection channel that handles electrical scrap.
☼
LIGHTING

Lamps, tubes & bulbs

LED bulbs, fluorescent tubes, CFLs and electronic lighting equipment.

Environmental concern

Some fluorescent lamps contain mercury and can release it if broken; electronic drivers also contain recoverable components.

Better disposalKeep lamps intact and use a collection route designed for lighting waste.
＋
SPECIALIST ELECTRONICS

Electronic instruments

Electronic measuring devices, non-infectious household health devices and instruments.

Environmental concern

Devices may include batteries, sensors or materials needing separate treatment. Clinical or contaminated equipment needs special handling.

Better disposalAsk the supplier or an authorized specialist about safe disposal, especially for clinical or contaminated items.
